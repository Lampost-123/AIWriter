# The Winter Ledger — story bible

The bible the book (story-v4.json, `--story book`) was written from, kept for anyone who changes it: an invented novel of 61,442 words, written as a fixed test story for a story-memory test. It must read as a real,
polished novel: quiet, wintry, wry and warm. Every fact below is deliberate: some are tested hundreds of pages later, so
they must be stated exactly as given, where given, and never contradicted. Ten writers each write one chapter (four
scenes) in parallel; consistency with this bible matters more than anything else.

## Premise

When the great clock of Callowmere stops on Midwinter Night, Tamsin Orr, bookkeeper of the clock-house of Orr &
Leckie, finds a sewn-shut page in her dead father's ledger: a debt of four hundred and twelve crowns to the guild. At
the guild's Reckoning on Thaw Day any house with an unpaid debt is forfeit, and the Warden of the Wharf, Corvin Slade,
wants the house. Tamsin travels four days north up the frozen River Varrow by dog-sledge to find her father's missing
partner, Edwin Leckie, who holds the second key to the guild vault and the proof that her father paid.

## Style (every writer, every scene)

- Close third person, past tense, always from Tamsin's point of view. Never enter anyone else's head: we only know what
  others think from what they say and do and how Tamsin reads them.
- British spelling (colour, grey, towards). Dialogue in single curly quotes ‘like this’, with curly apostrophes (’).
  Nested quotes in double curly quotes. Em dashes sparingly.
- No modern words or idiom (no "okay", "guys", "fine with it"). No chapter headings inside the text.
- Paragraphs vary, mostly 40–160 words; each speaker's line starts a new paragraph.
- Avoid stock phrases: "a testament to", "the weight of", "let out a breath she didn't know she was holding",
  "something shifted", "the silence stretched", "in that moment". Concrete detail over abstraction.
- Each scene 1,350–1,600 words. Each scene stands on its own and moves the story; end scenes mid-motion or on a line,
  not on a summing-up.
- Numbers in words ("four hundred and twelve crowns", "six dogs").

## Calendar

Day 1 is Midwinter Night (Lantern Night). Thaw Day, when the Reckoning sits, is day 47 (a fixed feast day). The
journey north starts on day 8; they reach Hollin on day 12; a blizzard and Leckie's illness hold them in Hollin until
day 41; they reach Callowmere again on day 45. Each scene card gives its day; keep to it.

## Places (geography is tested: never change it)

- **The River Varrow** flows SOUTH out of the northern hills to Callowmere and the sea. In deep winter it freezes into
  the ice road. Going north is going upriver; going home to Callowmere is going south, downriver.
- **Callowmere**: the river city at the mouth of the Varrow. The WEST bank has Gilt Street (the clockmakers' street:
  the clock-house of Orr & Leckie, Hesketh Fane's shop), the west wharf, and the guild pigeon loft on the roofs above
  the wharf. The EAST bank has the Guildhall with the great clock in its tower, and the guild vault in its cellars. The
  only way between them is the Chain Bridge, whose stone steps at the west end ice over in winter. The ice-road stand
  is on the frozen river just below the bridge. The north gate is where the bank road comes in.
- **Ambry**: a village one day north, with a mill and millrace on the west bank and an inn beside the mill.
- **Corran Weir**: two days north. A great stone weir across the river, with the thaw-bell tower and the weir-keeper's
  house on the west bank. Below the weir the ice is always thin.
- **Lake Dun and Dunmere**: three days north the river widens into Lake Dun; Dunmere is the town on its north shore,
  with the Heron inn, a pawnbroker on the quay, and Hanne Roe's dog yard.
- **Hollin**: four days north, a grey stone mining town climbing a steep valley above the head of Lake Dun. Mine-heads
  smoke above it. A chapel, the well at the top of the town, the Copper Kettle inn, Bettany Leckie's house below the
  chapel. Hollin folk speak Hollish, the hill tongue.
- **The bank road**: the old road along the west bank, used when the river ice is closed.

## Rules of the world (tested)

- **Guild pigeons** fly only by daylight, never after dark, and only ever home to their own loft. Tamsin's three birds
  come from the Callowmere loft, so they can only carry word home to Callowmere.
- **The thaw bell** at Corran Weir: when it rings, the Wardens close the ice road by law, and no runner may go out on
  the river again until next winter.
- **The guild vault** opens only to two keys turned together: the Orrs' key (Tamsin wears it on her belt) and Edwin
  Leckie's key.
- **The Reckoning**: every house's books are laid open on Thaw Day; a house with an unpaid debt to the guild is
  forfeit to the guild.

## People

- **Tamsin Orr** — 24, the protagonist. Bookkeeper of Orr & Leckie since her father died; raised her brother. Small,
  neat, black hair in a single plait, dark brown eyes. Careful with money, honest, sharp-tongued when frightened, dry
  humour. Right-handed. Speaks Hollish (from her mother). Cannot swim: at seven she fell into the millrace at Ambry and
  a miller's boy pulled her out by her hair; since then she cannot bear water over her knees.
- **Aldous Orr** — her father, dead. Clockmaker; built the great clock of the Guildhall with Edwin Leckie twelve years
  ago. Drowned eleven winters ago when the ice gave way under him below Corran Weir, coming home from Hollin with a
  sledge-load of brass for the house, hurrying to beat the thaw bell; his body was found in the spring. Tamsin was
  thirteen that winter. He had borrowed four hundred and twelve crowns from the guild that autumn to pay for the great
  clock's bells, and repaid it in full, in gold, to Slade (then the guild's treasurer) the week before he drowned;
  Slade signed a receipt and pocketed the gold.
- **Ysolde Orr** — her mother, dead. A Hollin woman; died of the marsh fever when Tamsin was nine. Taught Tamsin
  Hollish and Hollish songs. Left her a Hollish hymn book.
- **Pim Orr** — 17, Tamsin's brother, apprentice clockmaker. Tall, gangly, fair like their father, cheerful, talks to
  everyone, cannot keep a secret. A genius with mechanisms. CANNOT READ: letters slide about for him; numbers and clock
  faces he reads perfectly. Orla Finch reads him any message that comes to the loft.
- **Kit Ansel** — 28, a sledge driver from Dunmere. Big and broad, grey eyes, short brown beard, easy laugh, kind,
  stubborn. Speaks no Hollish beyond "thank you" and "how much". His younger brother Davey drowned through the ice of
  Lake Dun five years ago. His late mother used to say "the lake has a long patience", and his sledge is named for it:
  the Long Patience. He runs six dogs at first: Hob (lead), Fen, Bracken, Sloe, Rusk, and little Tansy at the back.
  From day 11 Tansy stays in Dunmere and he runs FIVE dogs: Hob, Fen, Bracken, Sloe, Rusk.
- **Hesketh Fane** — about 70, bonesetter and apothecary on Gilt Street, an old friend of Aldous. Thin, stooped, white
  whiskers, wry and gentle. His shop smells of camphor. His late wife Elsbet came from the hills and loved the
  snowbells of Hollin; she died three winters ago.
- **Corvin Slade** — about 55, Warden of the Wharf: the guild's highest officer, keeper of its books and the wharf
  dues. Tall, thin, courteous and cold. Pale, washed-blue eyes. Walks with a silver-headed cane for a stiff RIGHT knee
  (an old fall on the wharf steps). Never leaves Callowmere in winter. Eleven years ago he was the guild's treasurer.
- **Edwin Leckie** — 61, Aldous's partner. White beard cut square. Left-handed; the forefinger of his right hand lost
  its top joint to a lathe long ago. Gentle, frightened. Kept Aldous's receipt among his papers for eleven years
  without understanding it; this autumn, when Slade began pressing him about "the old debt" and threatening him, he
  understood, and fled north to his sister in Hollin with the receipt and his vault key. Ill with lung fever in
  Hollin. His heart is failing (only Isla and, from day 38, Tamsin know).
- **Bettany Leckie** — about 58, Edwin's widowed sister in Hollin. Brisk, suspicious, speaks both tongues.
- **Orla Finch** — about 45, keeper of the guild pigeon loft. Brusque and kind.
- **Dace Wilmot** — about 40, clerk to the guild and Slade's agent. Lean, bottle-green coat, ink-stained cuffs, a
  polite smile. Follows Tamsin north. Slade leaves him stranded and unpaid in Hollin; at the Reckoning he turns on
  Slade and testifies.
- **Isla Thorne** — about 80, Hollin's old healer. Speaks only Hollish, no lowland speech at all. Small, bent, bright
  black eyes. Knew Tamsin's mother as a girl.
- **Old Brannock** — about 70, keeper of Corran Weir. Pulled Aldous's sledge out of the river eleven years ago.
- **Hanne Roe** — the dog-healer at Dunmere, with her young daughter Mina.

## Who knows about the debt (tested, changes over time)

- Day 1 (s2): only Tamsin. She decides not to tell Pim, because Pim cannot keep a secret.
- Day 3 (s6): she tells Hesketh Fane.
- Day 10 (s16): she tells Kit, after he pulls her out of the river.
- Day 11 (s19): Dace Wilmot overhears her and Kit at the Heron, so Slade's side knows she knows.
- Day 13 (s24): Leckie tells her the truth (her father paid; Slade kept the gold).
- Day 45 (s35): she finally tells Pim everything.
- Isla's secret (s27): Leckie's heart is failing. Tamsin tells no one, ever, in this book.

## Things that change (tested)

- **Tamsin's right wrist**: broken on day 3 (s5); in a splint from day 3 (s6) until day 33 (s26), when Isla cuts the
  splint away; weak afterwards, strengthening; whole again by Thaw Day. While splinted she does everything left-handed
  and badly (her handwriting goes shaky), lifts nothing with the right.
- **The pigeons**: three (s7). First sent from Corran Weir on day 9 (s12): two left. Second sent from Dunmere at noon
  on day 11 (s18): one left. The last sent at first light on day 41 from the Hollin road (s29): none left. All three
  reach the Callowmere loft (s36).
- **The dogs**: six until Tansy is hurt (s15); Tansy rides on the sledge until Dunmere (s18) and stays there. Five from
  then on.
- **Her father's silver pocket watch** (her initials-engraved keepsake: "A.O." inside the lid): she carries it until
  she pawns it in Dunmere on day 11 (s18). On day 42 (s30) she learns the pawnbroker sold it; gone for good. After s18
  she never has a watch.
- **Leckie's vault key**: given to Tamsin on day 38 (s27); from day 40 (s28) hidden in a hollow Kit cuts in the heel of
  her LEFT boot, until she opens the vault at the Reckoning (s38).
- **The receipt**: given on day 38 (s27); sewn into the lining of her coat.
- **The snowbell**: promised to Fane on day 3 (s6); Isla gives Tamsin a pressed snowbell on day 38 (s27), kept inside
  her mother's Hollish hymn book; she gives it to Fane on day 48 (s40).
- **The ice road**: open until the thaw bell rings at Corran on day 43 (s31); after that they go by the bank road and
  never set a runner on the river ice again.
- **Dace Wilmot**: Slade's man until Hollin; at the Reckoning (s38) he turns and testifies against Slade (Slade left
  him unpaid to freeze in Hollin).

## Exact sentences

Each brief lists sentences that MUST appear in the scene word for word, character for character (same curly quotes,
apostrophes and punctuation), each as part of a paragraph. Weave them in naturally; you may add sentences around them
but never alter them.

## Marker sentences

Some scenes have a marker sentence: it must START a paragraph, word for word. Everything before that paragraph is
shown to a test on its own, so the brief says what may not be mentioned before it.

## Forbidden words

Each brief lists words or topics that must not appear anywhere in that scene (or only after the marker, where said).
These keep tested facts far from the test points. Obey them strictly, including in dialogue.
